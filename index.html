🧭 Payroll Compass

A private, client-side tool for validating Panamanian payroll deductions.

🎯 The Purpose

Understanding your paycheck can sometimes feel complex, especially when dealing with variable income components like bonuses, overtime, or the XIII Month payment. While your pay stub provides essential transaction details for each pay period, it can be challenging to visualize the "big picture" of how your cumulative year-to-date (YTD) income affects progressive taxes like ISR.

Payroll Compass is designed to complement the work your payroll department does by providing visual clarity and year-long projection capabilities. This tool helps you see beyond a single pay stub and understand the full-year tax trajectory—something that's difficult to convey in a standard biweekly pay statement.

💡 How It Helps

This calculator serves as a validation and education tool that allows you to:

  • Visualize the Full Year: See how your cumulative income and tax obligations evolve month-by-month, rather than viewing each paycheck in isolation.
  • Understand Progressive Taxation: Learn why your ISR deductions may feel "aggressive" in months with bonuses or higher income, and how they naturally ease off toward year-end as your YTD totals stabilize.
  • Validate Accuracy: Compare your actual deductions against the official Panamanian tax code formulas to confirm everything aligns with legal requirements.
  • Anticipate Cash Flow: Discover how year-end tax adjustments often result in higher net pay during the final pay periods—perfect timing for holiday shopping or year-end expenses.

🤝 Payroll Collaboration

This tool is designed to support and complement the excellent work of payroll departments. Payroll teams use sophisticated projection algorithms to ensure tax compliance throughout the year. These algorithms may withhold conservatively during high-income months (like when bonuses or XIII Month payments are issued) to prevent year-end tax shortfalls.

By understanding this year-long view, you can appreciate that what might initially seem like a larger-than-expected deduction is actually a prudent measure that "trues up" by year-end. In fact, many employees notice increased take-home pay in November and December as cumulative taxes align precisely with legal obligations—leaving a bit of extra cash on hand for the holidays.

We encourage employees to use this calculator alongside their pay stubs, and to reach out to their payroll department with any questions. The goal is transparency, understanding, and confidence in the payroll process.

🔒 Privacy & Data Security

🚨 This is the most important section. Please read carefully.

This tool follows a "Zero-Knowledge" architecture.

  • Client-Side Only: All calculations happen 100% inside your web browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari).
  • No Database: There is no backend server. We do not store your salary, your name, or your financial data.
  • No Transmission: When you click "Add Pay Stub," data moves from your keyboard to your browser's temporary memory (RAM). It is NEVER sent over the internet.
    • - The same is true when you "Import" your CSV backup file.
  • Session-Based: If you refresh the page, your data is gone forever—unless you explicitly choose to export a backup.

How to Verify

You can disconnect your internet after loading the page. The tool will continue to function perfectly, proving that it does not need to "phone home" to work.

📝 Getting Started

⚠️ Important: Always start your ledger with JANUARY of the current year. Do not enter previous year pay stubs as rows. For accurate XIII Month projections, configure carryover income using the gear icon (⚙️) in the XIII Estimator card.

1. Date

Select the pay stub date. The [XIII] toggle appears automatically during valid payment windows (Apr 1-15, Aug 1-15, Dec 1-15).

  • Enter your Base Salary (required field)
  • Toggle [QBP], [OT], or [VAC] to add variable income

📊 Tax Rules & Rates

All rates and brackets are based on the Panamanian Tax Code (Código Fiscal).

Social Security (SS)

Income Type Rate Notes
Base Salary 9.75% Applied to regular salary + overtime + vacation + extra
GREP (Representation) 9.00% Lower rate for representation allowance
XIII Month (13th Month) 7.25% Panamanian labor law rate for the 3 annual XIII Month payments (April, August, December)

Educational Tax (SE)

Calculation Base Rate
Base Salary + Overtime + Vacation + Extra 1.25%

Note: SE excludes GREP, Home Stipend, and QBP Bonuses

ISR Salary (Income Tax - Progressive)

Annual Income Range (YTD) Tax Rate
$0 - $11,000 0%
$11,000 - $50,000 15%
$50,000+ 25%

Progressive: Only the income within each bracket is taxed at that rate (e.g., if you earn $15,000 YTD, the first $11,000 is taxed at 0%, and only $4,000 is taxed at 15%)

ISR GREP (Income Tax on Representation - Progressive)

Annual GREP Income (YTD) Tax Rate
$0 - $25,000 10%
$25,000+ 15%

Note: GREP income is taxed separately from salary income using different brackets

💡 Calculation Examples

Step-by-step breakdowns to help you understand how taxes are calculated.

Example 1: Base Salary Only (Simple Case)
Income: Base Salary = $2,000
SS Calculation: $2,000 × 9.75% = $195.00
SE Calculation: $2,000 × 1.25% = $25.00
ISR: Depends on YTD cumulative (see progressive brackets)
Total Deductions (SS + SE + ISR): $195.00 + $25.00 + ISR
Example 2: Base Salary + GREP (Common Case)
Income: Base Salary = $2,000, GREP = $500
SS Calculation:
• Base: $2,000 × 9.75% = $195.00
• GREP: $500 × 9.00% = $45.00
• Total SS: $240.00
SE Calculation: $2,000 × 1.25% = $25.00 (GREP excluded)
ISR: Separate calculations for Salary ISR and GREP ISR
Note: GREP uses different rates for both SS (9.00%) and ISR (separate brackets)
Example 3: XIII Month Payment Only (Special Case)
Scenario: Separate XIII Month pay stub (typical payroll practice)
Income: XIII Salary = $1,500, XIII GREP = $400
Note: XIII amounts are calculated by averaging income over the qualifying period (e.g., Dec 16 - Apr 15) ÷ 12
SS Calculation:
• XIII Salary: $1,500 × 7.25% = $108.75
• XIII GREP: $400 × 7.25% = $29.00
• Total SS: $137.75
SE Calculation: XIII is excluded from SE, so SE = $0
ISR: Calculated normally based on cumulative YTD (same as regular paychecks)
Key: XIII Month uses the 7.25% SS rate as stipulated by Panamanian labor law. This rate applies to all 3 annual XIII payments.
Example 4: Progressive ISR (Year-to-Date Impact)
Scenario: YTD Salary = $10,000 after 5 paychecks, this paycheck adds $2,500
New YTD Total: $10,000 + $2,500 = $12,500
ISR Calculation:
• First $11,000: 0% = $0
• Remaining $1,500: 15% = $225
• Total ISR YTD: $225
• Previous ISR paid: $0 (was below $11k)
• ISR this paycheck: $225
This is why crossing a tax bracket can cause a larger deduction - you're "catching up" on the cumulative tax owed

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Why is my SS different when I have GREP?

A: GREP (Representation Allowance) is taxed at a lower SS rate (9.00%) compared to Base Salary (9.75%). This is mandated by Panamanian law. When you have both Base and GREP, your total SS deduction is calculated separately for each component and then summed. Hover over the SS value in the ledger to see the breakdown.

Q: How does progressive ISR work? Why did my deduction jump?

A: ISR (Income Tax) is calculated on your cumulative year-to-date (YTD) income, not just the current paycheck. The tax brackets are progressive, meaning different portions of your income are taxed at different rates. When your YTD total crosses a bracket threshold (e.g., $11,000 or $50,000), the amount above that threshold is taxed at the higher rate. This is why you might see a larger ISR deduction when you get a bonus or your YTD crosses into a new bracket.

Q: What's the difference between "Gross Salary" and "Total Earnings"?

A: Gross Salary is the sum of all taxable salary components (Base + Bonuses + Overtime + Vacation + XIII + Extra). This is the base for calculating SS, SE, and ISR on salary. Total Earnings includes Gross Salary plus GREP and Home Stipend. GREP and Home are treated differently for tax purposes (GREP has separate SS and ISR rates, Home is typically non-taxable).

Q: Why does my effective tax rate change each paycheck?

A: Your effective rate (percentage shown below each tax value) changes because ISR is cumulative. Early in the year, you may still be in the 0% bracket, so you see no ISR. As your YTD grows and crosses thresholds, more of your income is taxed at higher rates, increasing your effective percentage. The YTD columns show your running cumulative effective rate for the year.

Q: The ISR graph shows "Retained" vs "Projected". What does this mean?

A: The Red Line (Retained) shows what your company has actually deducted from your paychecks so far. The Green Line (Projected) shows what should be withheld based on an annualized projection of your year-end income. This projection method distributes the expected annual tax evenly across all pay periods, which is how most payroll departments actually operate. If Retained is above Projected, you're "ahead" on taxes - the company has withheld more than the annualized projection suggests. This smooths out your take-home pay throughout the year and prevents large fluctuations when bonuses or other variable income is received.

Q: What income types are excluded from SE (Educational Tax)?

A: SE is calculated at 1.25% on Base Salary + Overtime + Vacation + Extra only. It explicitly excludes: GREP, Home Stipend, and QBP Bonuses. This is why your SE rate appears lower than expected when you have these additional income types.

Q: My payroll deduction doesn't match the calculator. What should I do?

A: Small discrepancies can occur due to rounding differences or specific company accounting practices. If the difference is significant (more than a few dollars), double-check that you've entered all income components correctly (including toggling GREP, bonuses, etc.). If it still doesn't match, consult with your HR department - they may have additional context about special deductions or adjustments specific to your situation.

Q: What do the variance columns (VAR SS, VAR ISR, etc.) show?

A: Variance columns show the cumulative running balance between what was actually retained (withheld from your paycheck YTD) versus what the projected smooth distribution suggests should have been withheld by this point. Red values indicate you've paid more than projected so far (over-withheld), while green values indicate you've paid less than projected so far (under-withheld). This cumulative approach shows "Am I ahead or behind overall?" rather than period-specific spikes, which is more useful for understanding your overall tax position. The variance converges to $0 by year-end when projections match actuals.

Q: Is my data safe? Where is it stored?

A: Your data is 100% client-side - it exists only in your browser's temporary memory (RAM) and is never transmitted to any server. There is no database, no backend, no cloud storage. If you refresh the page, all data is permanently lost unless you export it using the Export button. This "zero-knowledge" architecture ensures maximum privacy. You can verify this by disconnecting from the internet after loading the page - it will continue to work perfectly.
